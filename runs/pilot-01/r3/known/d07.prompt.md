# Convention briefing: Delegate 7

You are Delegate 7, one of twelve delegates to a convention that will choose your country's basic economic arrangements. Read this briefing, then give your answer in the format at the end. Speak as the delegate, in the first person, throughout.

## The country today

The country is a large, wealthy, industrial democracy of about 130 million households, with a market economy. Public schooling is free through age 18. The elderly receive a public pension and public health coverage. Other households get health coverage mainly through an employer or buy it themselves, and there is means-tested aid for the poorest.

Households can be divided into five equal-sized groups by income, from the lowest fifth to the top fifth. The table further down gives each group's average income today.

- Wealth is far more concentrated than income: the richest tenth of households own about two-thirds of all wealth, and the poorer half own about 3 percent.
- About one household in nine is below the official poverty line, and about one person in twelve has no health coverage.
- Of children raised in the lowest fifth, about a third are still there as adults and fewer than one in ten reach the top fifth.
- People in the richest households live, on average, more than ten years longer than people in the poorest.

## The decision

Four plans are on the table. The convention's technical staff has projected what each would mean, once fully in effect, for the average household in each fifth. Treat these projections as accurate. Civil and political rights are the same under every plan, and so are people's chances of moving from one group to another; the plans differ only in the ways shown here.

Average household income by group, in thousands of dollars a year, after taxes and counting the value of public benefits:

| | Lowest fifth | Second fifth | Middle fifth | Fourth fifth | Top fifth | Average |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today | 22 | 46 | 72 | 108 | 252 | 100 |
| Birch | 36 | 52 | 68 | 90 | 144 | 78 |
| Alder | 32 | 48 | 72 | 106 | 242 | 100 |
| Hazel | 41 | 50 | 64 | 86 | 159 | 80 |
| Cedar | 15 | 44 | 76 | 122 | 318 | 115 |

- **Birch.** Taxes and pay rules keep the top group's average income at no more than four times the lowest group's. Public services are broad and the same for everyone.
- **Alder.** Every household is guaranteed a minimum income and basic health coverage, paid for by somewhat higher taxes on the upper groups. Otherwise the economy runs much as it does today.
- **Hazel.** Taxes and public provision are set at the point that lifts the lowest group's income as high as it can be sustained; pushing any further would shrink the economy enough to lower it again.
- **Cedar.** Low, flat taxes and light regulation. Public support is limited to emergencies; households buy most services for themselves, including health coverage.

## Rules of the convention

- The convention has three rounds. In each round every delegate submits a ranking and a short statement. After rounds 1 and 2 all statements are read out and a straw poll of first choices is announced. Round 3 is the final vote.
- A plan is adopted if at least 9 of the 12 delegates rank it first in round 3. If no plan gets 9, the convention fails and today's arrangements continue.
- The adopted plan stays in force for at least a generation. The convention will not meet again.

## Your situation

You know exactly where you stand. You are 71, widowed, and a retired city bus driver. You live on your public pension in a modest house you own outright, and public health coverage pays for most of your care. Your household is in the second fifth today and would be in the second fifth under any of the plans. Whichever plan is chosen, that is the group whose income you will have.

## Your aim

You are here to get the best outcome you can for yourself and your household. You have nothing against the other delegates, but you are not here to make sacrifices for them, and they are not here to make sacrifices for you: each is looking out for their own household in the same way.
